## What a "cheap" gaming PC actually costs

Price bands are roughly stable, even if the exact models rotate every few months.

| Budget | What you can realistically expect | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $500–$700 | 1080p at medium-to-high settings on most titles; integrated or entry-level discrete graphics | Refurb prebuilts, clearance last-gen towers |
| $800–$1,000 | 1080p high / 1440p medium; the sweet spot for most buyers | Sale-window prebuilts, DIY with a mid-range GPU |
| $1,000–$1,300 | 1440p high, room to upgrade later | DIY, or a prebuilt with a current-gen mid-tier GPU |
| $1,300+ | 1440p high refresh or entry 4K | Not really "cheap" anymore — this is a different search |

Two things move you down a band without sacrificing much. First, last-generation parts: a previous-gen CPU and GPU typically cut a few hundred dollars off a build while costing you modestly in frames. Second, refurbished and open-box stock. Retailers sell returned units at real discounts because they can't sell them as new, and a manufacturer-refurbished machine with a warranty is a different proposition from a used one off a marketplace.

Prebuilt versus DIY is less clear-cut than the internet suggests. DIY usually wins on price-per-component, but only if you already own a monitor, keyboard, and an operating system licence. Add those in and a sale-priced prebuilt often lands in the same range — with one warranty covering the whole box instead of five.

## The honest math on cashback

Here's where most cashback articles oversell. Cashback does not lower the price at checkout. You pay the full total, and a percentage of the eligible order value comes back to you later as real money, once the retailer confirms the transaction. On a $900 gaming PC at a 1–3% rate, that's roughly $9 to $27.

That is not a reason to skip it, but it is a reason to be accurate about it. Cashback matters most in three situations:

- **High-ticket electronics**, where even 1% is a meaningful dollar figure.
- **The accessories around the PC** — monitor, chair, desk, headset, storage. A 2% return across $1,500 of total spend is worth more than the cashback on the tower alone.
- **Stacking.** Cashback comes out of the retailer's marketing budget, your card rewards come from the card issuer, and the sale price comes from the retailer's pricing decision. None of the three cancels another, so the same purchase can earn on all three layers.

The mistake is treating cashback as the reason to buy. Treat it as the last click before checkout — the thing you do after you've already decided the price is acceptable.

Cashback is an attribution mechanic, not a discount code. A tracking cookie has to follow you from the cashback platform to the retailer and through to payment. Anything that interrupts that path can void the return — and there's no way to fix it after the fact, because the retailer never reported the sale as coming from the platform.

The conditions that keep it clean:

1. **Start at ShopBack, finish in the same session.** Don't open the retailer in one window and ShopBack in another.
2. **Stay on one device.** Starting in the app and paying on desktop (or the reverse) is a common way to lose tracking.
3. **Turn off ad blockers, privacy extensions and VPNs**, or allowlist the platform and the retailer.

5. **Only use promo codes the platform lists.** Codes from third-party coupon sites can overwrite the tracking cookie and void the cashback entirely.
6. **Don't detour to other shops, coupon sites or search engines between activation and payment.** That's the most common culprit on a big purchase.
7. **Accept all cookies** on the retailer's site, and keep the purchase inside the market you're shopping from.
8. **For high-value electronics, be extra careful.** One order per click-through; if payment fails, restart from the platform.

## A realistic order of operations

If the goal is genuinely a cheap gaming PC rather than a clever-looking purchase, sequence it like this.

**Set a target price before you shop.** Note the normal price of two or three candidate machines or component lists. When a sale opens, compare against that baseline, not against the retailer's "was" price.

**Decide the channel.** Refurbished and open-box machines from a retailer with a warranty are usually the cheapest legitimate route to a given spec. DIY wins if you already own the peripherals and an OS licence. Sale-window prebuilts win if you want one warranty and no assembly.

**Buy around the sale windows** — late November through Cyber Monday is where laptop and gaming-category pricing is deepest, and boosted cashback rates tend to run alongside the retailer events. For a build you can wait on, that window is usually worth waiting for.

**Then route the checkout.** Log in, search the retailer, click through, complete the purchase in one uninterrupted session, and don't touch another coupon site on the way.

**Track and confirm.** Check that the transaction shows up as pending within a couple of days. If it doesn't, file a missing cashback claim from inside your account with the order number, date and amount.
